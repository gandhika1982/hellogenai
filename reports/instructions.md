# How to Fill Out the Project Status Report

Use [template.md](./template.md) to prepare a concise update for the reporting period. Replace every bracketed prompt with project-specific information, and remove unused duplicate rows or bullets. If a section has nothing to report, write “None identified” or “None” rather than leaving it ambiguous.

## Before you start

1. Set a clear reporting period and use the same dates throughout the report.
2. Identify the project, report owner, and date the report was prepared.
3. Gather evidence from the team’s completed work, current plan, milestone dates, open risks, and outstanding decisions.

## Choose an overall status

- **Green / On track:** Work is progressing within the agreed plan; no material issue currently threatens a committed milestone.
- **Amber / At risk:** A meaningful risk or dependency could affect a milestone, scope, or quality unless action is taken. State the mitigation and when the status will be reviewed.
- **Red / Off track:** A milestone or agreed outcome is already missed or is unlikely to be met without a change. State the impact and the decision or recovery action required.

Base the status on outcomes and evidence, not effort or optimism. Explain Amber or Red in the summary and connect it to an entry in Risks and Issues.

## Complete each section

- **Summary:** Write 2–4 sentences for a reader who may only scan this section. State the status, the most important progress, the outlook, and any major concern.
- **Completed this period:** List observable outcomes delivered during the period and their impact. Do not list routine activity without a result.
- **Planned for next period:** List the most important expected outcomes. Use specific, verifiable deliverables where possible.
- **Risks and Issues:** Separate a possible future risk from a problem already occurring. Describe the impact, actionable mitigation or next step, a named owner, and a due date. If none exist, say “None identified.”
- **Decisions and Help Needed:** Make each request explicit: what is needed, who should provide it, and by when. If no request is outstanding, say “None.”
- **Milestones:** Include relevant project commitments with target dates and status. Use “At risk” when a milestone may slip, and explain why in Notes or Risks and Issues.

## Final checks

- Dates, milestone statuses, and the overall status agree across sections.
- Every Amber/Red status, material risk, or milestone concern has an owner and next action.
- Decisions and requests name a responsible person or group and a needed-by date.
- The report distinguishes completed outcomes from planned work.
- The report contains no confidential information that its intended recipients should not see.
- Remove instructions and placeholders before sharing the completed report.
